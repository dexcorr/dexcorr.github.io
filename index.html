DexCorr: Force-Aware Local Takeovers for Robust Dexterous Manipulation

Anonymous Authors

DexCorr uses force-aware local takeovers to make dexterous manipulation robust to contact-rich failures.

Abstract

Dexterous manipulation with multi-fingered hands remains brittle under contact-rich interactions, where small pose errors and unmodeled forces compound into failures. We present DexCorr, a framework that combines learned policies with force-aware local takeovers: a corrective controller that monitors contact forces and momentarily assumes control at critical moments to stabilize the interaction before returning control to the base policy.

By reasoning about force signals rather than pose alone, DexCorr detects incipient slips and misalignments and applies targeted corrections that recover the task without disrupting the overall behavior. This yields more robust and reliable manipulation across a range of contact-rich tasks.

We evaluate DexCorr on a suite of dexterous manipulation tasks and show that it substantially improves success rates and robustness compared to policies without force-aware correction.
